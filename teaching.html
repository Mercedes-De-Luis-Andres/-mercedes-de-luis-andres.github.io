Teaching

Visual Reporting: The Art of Journalism

Masaryk University, Faculty of Social Studies · Autumn 2026

This seminar explores visual journalism as a culturally situated, empathetic and ethical practice. It moves beyond traditional paradigms of detached objectivity to examine the subjective turn in visual reporting through social sciences, the ethics of care and collective memory.

Structured around The Art of Being, The Art of Framing and The Art of Seeing, the course combines critical theory with hands-on practice. Students work with the sketch and the written notebook as tools for observation, knowledge and social memory, while exploring ethical questions surrounding visual representation, power and the construction of meaning.

The course brings together slow observation, field notes, contextualized photography and narrative practices. Readings include John Berger, Umberto Eco, Hugo Pratt, Susan Sontag and Joan Tronto.

Lecturas Compartidas

VHS Wiener Urania · Autumn 2026

A Spanish-language reading circle for adult learners (B1–C1), developed as a public-education adaptation of the BoJoClub approach.

The course offers moderated shared-reading sessions focused on Spanish-language literature. It represents an initial step in transferring a methodology developed through journalism education into community-based learning beyond the university.

Visual Reporting

University of Klagenfurt · Spring 2024

An English-language seminar exploring the convergence of journalism and image through a humanist perspective, engaging with authors including Susan Sontag, John Berger and Hugo Pratt.

The course combined visual literacy, ethical reflection and inclusive storytelling with collective reading and discussion practices.

Travel Journalism

University of Klagenfurt · Winter 2024

An English-language course examining travel narratives within literary journalism and digital media, from print culture to emerging formats.

The course focused on slow journalism, representations of “the exotic” and the relationship between media and public perception. A shared reading component, structured as a book club, formed part of the methodology.
